## The 18 frames that were wrong, and what caught them

**`--build` is not optional when the scenarios are new.** J4, J5 and J6 captured Android without
it, against an APK that predated their scenario keys. The app did not fail — it ignored the unknown
key and fell through to its normal launch path, so **18 frames photographed "Please sign in again —
Your Omen session expired"** instead of the journey. Every one rendered cleanly. Every one was the
wrong screen.

**No visual review would reliably have caught this**, because each frame looks like a legitimate
screenshot of a real state. What caught it was **md5 across the set**: six different J4 scenarios
produced one byte-identical PNG, four J5 scenarios another, two J6 pairs two more. Two different
screens cannot produce the same bytes. Byte size alone was the first hint — identical sizes across
different scenarios — and hashing confirmed it in one command.

Re-captured against a fresh APK; duplicates fell from six hashes to one.

**Add this to the capture routine**: after any batch, hash the set and investigate every collision
before looking at a single frame. It is one command and it is strictly stronger than eyes for this
failure mode.

## The one remaining duplicate is correct

`journey-j2.{nominal,degraded}.03-switch-loading` are byte-identical **on iOS** — and should be.
`SwitchLoading` is the mid-switch state and `CONTRACTS.md` §10.3 requires that "the previous team's
numbers are discarded, never reused while loading". A screen holding no data has nothing to
degrade, so its degraded pass is legitimately the same picture. The frame renders that rule
literally: *"Reading Davante's Inferno from ESPN. The previous team's numbers are gone, not
reused."* The Android pair differs by 14 bytes — a clock digit.
